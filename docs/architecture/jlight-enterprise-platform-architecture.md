# JLIGHT Health Intelligence System — Enterprise Platform Architecture

**Status:** Approved staging architecture specification  
**Applies to:** Organisation/workspace lifecycle, Hospital, Laboratory, Bioinformatics, Clinical Trials, Patient Journey, recruitment linkage, AI intelligence, data and security architecture  
**Production:** No production code, schema, data, configuration, merge, or deployment changes are authorised by this specification.

## 1. Product definition and design objective

JLIGHT is a production-oriented, AI-enabled Health Intelligence System. It is an intelligence layer that operates above and alongside existing health, laboratory, genomics and clinical-research systems. It ingests authorised records and events, makes them interoperable, evaluates them continuously or on an appropriate processing schedule, detects meaningful changes and risks, and produces explainable, traceable Decision Cards for the people authorised to act.

JLIGHT is not a laboratory instrument, analyser, specimen-processing system, sequencing instrument, diagnostic device, or replacement for an LIS, HIS, LIMS, CTMS, EHR or specialist bioinformatics pipeline. It does not collect, prepare, test, physically analyse, process or sequence specimens, control instruments, or generate primary measurements. It analyses information generated by those systems and workflows.

The product objective is not to reproduce existing dashboards. JLIGHT must provide a higher-level intelligence capability: connect fragmented operational context, detect relationships that are difficult to see in individual systems, prioritise important exceptions, explain why a signal matters, show its evidence and limitations, and support timely human decisions.

## 2. Organisation and workspace model — based on the ChatGPT Business pattern

JLIGHT uses one organisation identity and one organisation workspace, not separate organisation registrations for each pathway.

### Initial organisation lifecycle
1. An organisation registers once and establishes its organisation identity, owner and workspace.
2. During initial registration, the organisation selects one or more pathways: Hospital, Laboratory, Bioinformatics and/or Clinical Trials.
3. JLIGHT creates one organisation code and records the requested/approved pathway entitlements against that organisation.
4. The owner invites members and assigns member permissions within the organisation's approved pathways.
5. On subsequent sign-in, a member chooses from the pathways already assigned to that organisation and to that member. The server verifies the selection; a browser selection, user metadata or organisation code is never authoritative.

### Governance and access
- Organisation-level entitlement answers: which pathways may this organisation operate?
- Member-level permission answers: which actions and records may this particular user access within an entitled pathway?
- Workspace selection is a navigation/context choice, not a permission grant.
- An organisation administrator cannot grant itself a new pathway entitlement.
- Additional pathways requested after initial registration require an independent JLIGHT Platform Administrator decision. A pending request grants no access. The review and decision must be auditable, including reviewer, time, rationale and policy version.
- Initial pathway activation must be governed by an explicit eligibility policy. The system must not imply that a pathway is approved merely because it was selected on a registration form.
- Email-code authentication verifies identity. It does not establish organisation membership, pathway entitlement, record permission or trial consent.
- An organisation code identifies an organisation; it is not a password, invitation, membership grant or access credential.
- Do not copy ChatGPT's role names mechanically. Use JLIGHT roles that represent the actual operational and governance responsibilities.

This model supports one organisation operating multiple pathways without duplicating its identity, members, billing relationship, audit history or organisation-level administration.

## 3. Operational workspaces and connected care

Hospital, Laboratory, Bioinformatics and Clinical Trials are distinct operational workspaces with their own domain records, workflows, members, permissions and reporting. A pathway may operate independently where the organisation has only that entitlement.

### Routine-care Patient Journey
Patient Journey is a controlled intelligence and traceability layer across Hospital, Laboratory and Bioinformatics. It is not a fourth operational workspace and it does not copy entire source databases. It links references to relevant records and events for the same person and care episode, where matching is reliable and the organisation and user have the required entitlements and record permissions.

Typical events may include hospital registration/admission and clinical orders; laboratory orders, accession and supplied specimen-status events, QC status, validation, result dispatch and critical-result acknowledgement; and authorised bioinformatics job, QC and report references. The sequence is not mandatory. Partial deployments must work.

Matching must use governed identifiers and context: verified patient key, encounter/admission ID, order/accession, specimen reference, source-system record ID, facility and event time. Weak demographic similarity must never silently merge records. Ambiguous matches go to an authorised reconciliation workflow. Journey views, exports, notifications and Decision Cards must enforce permissions for each underlying source record.

## 4. Hospital-to-Clinical-Trial recruitment connection

The Hospital and Clinical Trials workspaces are operationally separate but intentionally connected through a governed **Patient-to-Trial Recruitment Link**. This is required because trial candidates are often recruited from hospital populations and some hospitals contain clinical-trial sites.

### Recruitment lifecycle
1. An existing hospital patient may be identified as a *potential candidate* for one or more studies, but only under an approved study recruitment process.
2. JLIGHT may assist authorised users by comparing permitted, minimum-necessary patient attributes against protocol-approved recruitment criteria. The AI may prioritise records for human review; it must not diagnose, make a final eligibility decision, enrol a person, or contact a patient autonomously.
3. An authorised hospital/research user creates a referral or recruitment hand-off to a named study and authorised trial site. The record includes provenance, purpose, referring user, time, status and permitted information scope.
4. The receiving research team accesses only the minimum information permitted for recruitment and screening under the study's ethics approval, lawful basis, site governance and applicable consent requirements.
5. Screening is performed by authorised research personnel using the approved protocol. AI support is advisory and explainable; it does not replace investigator judgement or protocol requirements.
6. Informed consent is obtained and documented according to the approved study process before enrolment and before any study procedure for which consent is required.
7. If enrolled, the person receives a study-specific participant identifier. Trial events are recorded against that participant identity, study and site.
8. Referral declined, not contacted, ineligible, withdrawn before enrolment, enrolled and other lifecycle states must be represented distinctly. A referral is not enrolment.

### Identity and access boundary
- The hospital patient identity and study-specific participant identity are different identities with different purposes.
- A secure, restricted linkage may relate them when the approved recruitment purpose and governance permit it. The link must be explicit, auditable, revocable/correctable where appropriate, and visible only to specifically authorised roles.
- No automatic identity merge, broad patient-record copy, implicit cross-domain access or unrestricted trial-team access to hospital records is permitted.
- Trial staff receive only the approved recruitment/screening subset and trial-domain records. Access to full hospital records remains separately authorised by the Hospital workspace.
- A participant may have more than one study relationship; participant identity is study-specific. Do not assume one universal participant ID across protocols.
- Trial visit, consent, deviation, AE/SAE and retention records must be keyed to the internal trial participant record and study context, not joined by searching the hospital patient registry.
- A hospital-to-trial linkage must not create a workspace entitlement or member permission.

The existing jl_participants.patient_id relationship and the separate jl_trial_participants model must be reviewed before further implementation. The staging design must establish one canonical, secure recruitment-link model and a controlled migration/compatibility plan; do not silently rely on the current direct foreign key or create a second competing participant registry.

## 5. Clinical Trial Participant Timeline

Clinical Trials has its own **Clinical Trial Participant Timeline**. It is not the routine Patient Journey with a filter applied.

The timeline is built from trial-domain events associated with the internal trial participant record and study: referral hand-off where visible to trial users, screening milestones, enrolment/status, protocol and consent milestones, scheduled/completed/missed visits, deviations, AE/SAE follow-up, retention signals and study close-out.

The recruitment provenance may show that the candidate came from a particular hospital/site and referral, but only the minimum authorised metadata. It must not expose unrelated routine-care encounters, laboratory results or bioinformatics records.

The routine Patient Journey and Clinical Trial Participant Timeline require separate navigation destinations, data loaders, APIs/RPC scopes, report generation, exports, search and access checks. Cross-domain linkage is surfaced only through the dedicated governed recruitment-link workflow, not by sharing the two timelines or joining them by demographic attributes.

## 6. AI and intelligence architecture

AI is a core system capability, not a chat widget or cosmetic addition. The architecture uses multiple complementary intelligence methods. A generative model is not the source of truth and must never invent clinical or operational evidence.

### Intelligence pipeline
1. **Connectors and ingestion:** receive authorised records/events from available LIS, HIS/EHR, LIMS, CTMS, HL7/FHIR feeds, genomics pipelines, structured imports and approved manual entry. Each connector has explicit capabilities and a health/status record.
2. **Validation and quarantine:** authenticate sources; validate schema, identifiers, tenant and pathway; detect malformed, duplicate, delayed, out-of-order or corrected messages; quarantine invalid data; record outcomes.
3. **Canonical data and provenance:** map source-specific records into versioned domain contracts while preserving source IDs, tenant/site, source system, event time, ingestion time, payload/version hashes, data quality, transformation version and lineage.
4. **Storage and processing:** retain source records according to approved retention rules; process high-volume events asynchronously and incrementally; support scheduled/batch processing where streaming is unavailable; provide replay and idempotency; isolate tenant and domain data.
5. **Deterministic and statistical engines:** apply validated rules, thresholds, SPC, Westgard logic, delta checks, time-window checks, trend analysis, risk scores and configured protocol logic.
6. **Machine-learning intelligence:** where sufficient validated data exists, use anomaly detection, forecasting, clustering and predictive models with documented training data, validation, calibration, operating limits and drift monitoring.
7. **Reasoning and synthesis:** combine engine outputs and evidence into concise explanations. Generative AI may summarise and prioritise verified findings, but it must cite the underlying record references, rule/model version and limitations. It must not fabricate values, diagnoses, causes, standards or recommended actions.
8. **Decision Cards and human workflow:** create one focused issue per card with severity, detected condition, what changed, evidence/provenance, applicable rule/standard and version, confidence/uncertainty, limitations, recommended human review, owner/acknowledgement and full audit history.

### Proposal-aligned intelligence engines
- Laboratory: Westgard QC signals, delta-check, predictive QC drift, run-in-control status, result/order consistency and supplied specimen traceability events.
- Hospital: completeness signals, critical-result acknowledgement and Patient Journey gaps, turnaround-time monitoring, HAI cluster signals and ward/workflow patterns.
- Clinical Trials: protocol visit windows, consent/protocol linkage, SAE reporting clock, deviation follow-up, recruitment funnel and retention/LTFU risk.
- Bioinformatics: NGS coverage, mapping, Q-score, duplication, GC content, contamination and antimicrobial-resistance pipeline output quality signals.
- Cross-domain: controlled patient/care-episode linkage, recruitment hand-off traceability, operational bottlenecks and data-quality inconsistencies.

Published standards and thresholds must be configurable, versioned and validated for the relevant assay, protocol, facility and jurisdiction. A threshold crossing is a signal for review, not automatically a confirmed error, diagnosis or root cause.

### AI governance
- Keep a registry of model, rule, feature and threshold versions and their validation status.
- Log the evidence and exact model/rule version behind each Decision Card.
- Monitor false positives, false negatives, calibration, drift, missingness and performance by relevant site/population where lawful and appropriate.
- Require human review for clinical, eligibility, safety and other consequential decisions.
- Prevent generative systems from accessing data outside the requesting user's verified tenant, workspace, purpose and record permissions.
- Use de-identified or synthetic data for development and testing unless an approved lawful process authorises otherwise.
- Do not send protected health information to external AI providers without explicit approved data-processing, security, residency and governance controls.
- Provide clear uncertainty, data freshness and source completeness indicators.

## 7. Enterprise data scale, resilience and integration

JLIGHT must be designed for high-volume, multi-organisation and multi-site operation rather than a single small dataset.

- Use tenant-scoped identifiers and enforced organisation/workspace/record-level authorisation on every API, query, job, report, export and AI operation.
- Separate operational transactional workloads from analytical/feature-processing workloads where volume requires it. Do not run expensive model scans synchronously in a user-facing request.
- Use asynchronous queues/workers for ingestion, enrichment, model execution, report generation and retries; implement idempotency, bounded retries, dead-letter handling, backpressure and replay.
- Partition/index high-volume event data by tenant, domain, site and time as justified by measured workload; use pagination and bounded queries.
- Store large raw files and pipeline artefacts in controlled object storage with metadata and provenance in the relational database. Avoid storing large blobs in browser local storage.
- Cache derived, permission-scoped summaries only where invalidation and tenant isolation are guaranteed.
- Support incremental processing, data freshness monitoring, connector health, processing lag, job status and recoverable failures.
- Maintain backups, tested restore procedures, retention schedules, disaster recovery objectives and audit-log integrity.
- Support offline/poor-connectivity workflows only where explicitly designed; queue local changes securely and reconcile conflicts without silently overwriting authoritative source records.
- Do not claim live, five-minute or real-time integration unless the connector, deployment, monitoring and site configuration have been implemented and verified. The proposal's five-minute cadence is a target for supported feeds, not a universal guarantee.
- Do not send commands to instruments or source clinical systems as part of this intelligence architecture.

## 8. Security, privacy, audit and governance

Enforce identity, organisation membership, pathway entitlement, member permission, record permission, purpose and tenant/site scope as separate checks. RLS is defence in depth; privileged functions and backend endpoints must independently validate callers and scope. Never rely on browser state, user-editable metadata, organisation code, or a model-generated answer for authorisation.

Maintain tamper-evident, append-only audit events for access-sensitive actions, recruitment referrals, linkage creation/correction/revocation, screening transitions, consent milestones, model/rule executions, Decision Card acknowledgements, report generation and administrative entitlement decisions. Audit records should identify actor, organisation, pathway, purpose, target record, action, timestamp, outcome and relevant version/hash without unnecessarily duplicating sensitive clinical content.

Clinical-trial recruitment and data use must follow protocol, ethics approval, lawful basis, site governance, consent requirements and applicable privacy law. AI matching must be limited to approved criteria and permitted data. Data minimisation, role separation, retention and breach response are mandatory.

## 9. Staging implementation and acceptance gates

1. Preserve production main, production database, production secrets and production deployment. All work in this task is staging/PR only.
2. Reconcile the two existing participant models (jl_participants and jl_trial_participants) and the existing patient linkage/audit tables before choosing a canonical implementation.
3. Inventory all participant, study, protocol, visit, consent, deviation, AE/SAE, patient, hospital-site, referral and membership fields, constraints, RLS policies, API scopes and frontend payload mappings.
4. Design and implement the governed patient-to-trial recruitment-link entity and lifecycle, with a minimum-necessary disclosure policy and explicit permission checks.
5. Separate the Clinical Trial Participant Timeline from routine Patient Journey in navigation, UI, data loaders, APIs, reports, exports and tests.
6. Remove patient-registry joins from the trial timeline. Trial event records must resolve through trial participant IDs and study scope. Patient linkage may be used only by the dedicated recruitment-link workflow.
7. Implement AI-assisted candidate prioritisation only after approved protocol criteria, permitted data scope, ethics/site governance and human review are defined. AI cannot make final eligibility or enrolment decisions.
8. Implement the ingestion, canonical event, provenance, processing and Decision Card contracts in a versioned, testable way. Record which connectors are actually available.
9. Test tenant isolation, multi-path organisation boundaries, hospital-to-trial referral permissions, consent state, study-specific IDs, multiple studies, ambiguous patient matching, link revocation/correction, timeline isolation, report/export permissions and audit completeness using synthetic staging data.
10. Test large-volume behaviour with representative synthetic loads, including ingestion throughput, queue lag, pagination, replay, duplicate events, failure recovery and model processing latency.
11. Run authenticated browser end-to-end tests and adversarial security review of privileged RPCs before considering release.
12. Document unmet connectors, eligibility policy, regulatory/ethics dependencies and performance limits accurately. Do not describe unimplemented capability as complete.
13. Production migration, merge to main and production deployment require a separate explicit release decision.

## 10. Non-goals and safety boundaries

- No physical specimen collection, preparation, testing, processing or sequencing by JLIGHT.
- No instrument/analyser control, calibration or primary result generation.
- No replacement of LIS, HIS/EHR, LIMS, CTMS or specialist pipelines.
- No autonomous diagnosis, treatment, final trial eligibility decision or enrolment.
- No automatic patient/participant identity merge or unrestricted cross-workspace data sharing.
- No AI access beyond verified tenant, workspace, record and purpose permissions.
- No unsupported claims of live connectivity, five-minute processing, regulatory certification or standards compliance.
- No production change as part of staging architecture work.

## 11. Relationship to the Executive Proposal

This architecture preserves the proposal's central design: existing LIS, HIS, CTMS, LIMS, HL7/FHIR, genomics and other permitted inputs feed the JLIGHT Intelligence Layer; proposal-aligned engines evaluate operational and scientific records; and Decision Cards deliver focused, traceable intelligence to authorised users. It also preserves the proposal's Patient Journey and trial intelligence while making the hospital-to-trial recruitment pathway explicit and governed.

The proposal's five-minute/real-time language is treated as a deployment target conditional on actual connector availability, not as a claim that every site already has live integration. The platform must accurately distinguish implemented, configured, simulated and planned capabilities.
